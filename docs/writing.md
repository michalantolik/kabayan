# Public Writing and Localization

## Purpose

This guide defines the initial public voice and multilingual editorial model for Discovery Landing
V1 and later public content. It is intentionally small. The project needs clear, trustworthy copy,
not a content-governance system.

The guidance is informed by the calm, evidence-based writing in `antolik`, adapted for an early
discovery project speaking with Filipinos rather than an engineer explaining completed work.

## Voice

Write in a way that is:

- calm, clear, warm, human, friendly, and respectful;
- specific, modest, trustworthy, and practical;
- honest about what is known, planned, and still uncertain.

Start with the real point. Prefer ordinary words, short paragraphs, and one main idea per paragraph
where practical. Explain an unfamiliar term when it is necessary. Do not make a simple idea sound
complicated, or make a complicated idea inaccurate merely to make it sound simple.

During discovery, the project should usually speak from this position:

```text
we do not know yet
        |
        v
we ask and listen
        |
        v
we look for recurring problems
        |
        v
we choose carefully
        |
        v
we build something small and see whether it helps
```

Prefer honest statements such as “We don't want to guess what Filipinos need” and “We're starting
by listening” over claims that the project already understands people's experiences.

Avoid corporate, bureaucratic, governmental, legalistic, academic, promotional, startup-like,
artificially inspirational, or paternalistic language. Do not sound like an employment agency,
immigration consultancy, charity speaking down to people, or authority speaking for Filipinos.
Avoid filler, fake enthusiasm, exaggerated empathy, marketing urgency, and importance manufactured
through emotional language.

## A useful writing instinct

Use this as an instinct, not a mandatory four-part template:

```text
CONCRETE
   |
   v
EXPLAIN
   |
   v
WHY IT MATTERS
   |
   v
NEXT NATURAL STEP
```

Distinguish fact, observation, interpretation, plan, and uncertainty. Make claims only as strong as
the evidence. Explain why something matters, then move to the next useful idea without adding a
ceremonial introduction or conclusion.

Use the smallest representation that communicates the idea. Plain prose is usually enough. Use a
small diagram for a real flow or relationship, and a compact table for repeated comparisons. Do
not add either merely to make the content look substantial.

## Three complete public languages

Discovery Landing V1 and future public content should ultimately have complete versions in:

- English;
- Filipino;
- Polish.

These are three versions of the same project, not separate personas. They must preserve the same:

- meaning and factual claims;
- evidence and product maturity;
- confidence and uncertainty;
- intent and respect for the reader.

They do not need identical sentences, paragraphs, idioms, or word order.

## Editorial and translation model

English is the current canonical meaning source. This creates one editorial reference and limits
drift; it does not make English linguistically superior or more important than Filipino.

The current editorial sequence is:

```text
English — canonical meaning
    |
    +--> Filipino — natural reviewed version
    |
    +--> Polish — natural reviewed version

same meaning, evidence, confidence, uncertainty, and intent
```

Translate meaning and voice, not sentence structure. When literal translation sounds unnatural,
prefer natural target-language writing as long as the substance and strength of every claim remain
equivalent. A translation must not quietly make the project more certain, authoritative,
promotional, cautious, or mature than another version.

Filipino public copy requires human language review before it is treated as reviewed public
content. AI may assist a later draft, but technical correctness does not establish natural usage.
Pilot 0 can help detect unnatural or overly formal wording, unexpected connotations, strange tone,
and naming associations. One reviewer improves the copy but does not establish a universal
Filipino language preference or become an authority for all Filipinos.

Polish should be authored as natural Polish public writing. Avoid literal English structures,
institutional vocabulary, marketing language, and exaggerated emotion when simpler natural Polish
expresses the same meaning.

The editorial sequence `English -> Filipino -> Polish` is a workflow, not a ranking of language
importance. A public selector order such as `FIL · EN · PL` is only a UI hypothesis. Confirm that
Filipino users understand and expect `FIL` before freezing the label or order during implementation
and Pilot 0.

## Let public writing become the reference

This document supplies the initial rules. Once reviewed, high-quality public copy exists, future
writers and agents should read it before drafting new content. Over time, the reviewed body of
public writing should become the strongest evidence of the project's real voice.

Preserve the voice and principles, not earlier sentence patterns. Do not turn successful copy into
a verbal template.

## Brand wording

Use **Kabayan Poland** consistently for the current public Poland experience. Do not describe it as a working name and do not reintroduce candidate-name voting. The broader repository/project identity may be **Kabayan** in technical and internal contexts.

Do not let the name make the copy sound like a recruitment agency, government service, legal authority, or guaranteed-help service unless future evidence and the actual product justify such a role.

## Compact copy review

### Substance

- Is this based on something we know, and is the point worth saying?
- Does any hypothesis appear as fact or claim understanding we do not have?

### Clarity and tone

- Can the reader understand the point quickly, with each paragraph doing a clear job?
- Can anything be simpler or shorter without losing meaning?
- Does it sound calm, human, respectful, and free of institutional, promotional, or fake-empathy
  language?

### Trust

- Are privacy statements accurate and uncertainty honest?
- Does the copy avoid promises we cannot guarantee and any implication of legal, immigration,
  employment, administrative, tax, or financial authority?

### Localization

- Does each version sound natural while preserving meaning, evidence, confidence, uncertainty,
  intent, and product maturity?
- Did translation strengthen or weaken a claim, and has Filipino copy received human review?
- Could wording or a name carry a cultural meaning we have missed?

Final question:

> Does this make the project clearer, more trustworthy, and easier to understand without claiming
> more than we know?
