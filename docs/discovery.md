# Discovery

## Objective

Discover which problems are real, repeated, important, and insufficiently solved before choosing
the first product capability.

## Public discovery loop

The public explanation should stay simple and describe actual behavior:

```text
LISTENING       Ask Filipinos about real experiences.        <- CURRENT
    |
    v
LEARNING        Look for problems that happen repeatedly.
    |
    v
CHOOSING        Choose one problem we can realistically help with.
    |
    v
BUILDING        Build the smallest useful solution.
    |
    v
LEARNING AGAIN  Check whether it helps, then decide what to do next.
    |
    +--------------------------------------------------------> repeat
```

The exact public copy may improve after Filipino-language review. Preserve the model: listen,
learn, choose, build, learn again.

## Current discovery sequence

1. Keep the respondent-ready survey foundation available for the pilot.
2. Implement and responsively validate the aligned Discovery Landing V1 direction.
3. Run Pilot 0: let one trusted Filipino reviewer explore the page before a detailed explanation
   where practical.
4. Record only anonymized or aggregated learning; refine the prototype and survey where justified.
5. Share with a few Filipinos in a small pilot, then learn and improve before broader sharing.
6. Combine aggregate patterns with useful follow-up conversations and observation.
7. Choose one problem only when evidence justifies testing it.
8. Test the smallest useful response through real use, then learn again.

Pilot 0 is intended to catch obvious problems in spontaneous understanding, trust, wording,
cultural or naming associations, mobile usability, actions, and unnecessary or misleading
content. It is not approval, validation, or representation of the Filipino community.

## Discovery mechanisms and limits

The landing prototype can reveal whether people understand and trust the invitation, can use it on
a phone, and react meaningfully to its language and naming. Its existence, visual appeal, or a
positive name reaction does not prove that the underlying product is needed.

The survey can reveal reported experiences and patterns worth investigating. It is not proof of
demand, product validation by itself, a product requirement generator, or a substitute for
conversations and observed behavior.

## What we want to learn

We want to learn what people have found difficult, what happened in a recent real situation, how
they tried to solve it, which alternatives they used, whether it was resolved, and what they wish
had been easier. Past behavior and repeated concrete experiences are stronger evidence than
hypothetical enthusiasm.

People should be able to describe problems in their own words before being offered categories for
analysis. Counts can reveal patterns worth investigating, but counts alone do not prove demand.
Follow-up conversations can explain why a pattern exists and what happened in real situations.

## Evidence discipline

- Assumptions and planning decisions are not market evidence.
- Survey responses are evidence, but not automatic product requirements.
- One person's experience is useful context, not validation for a community.
- Naming preference is not validation of a problem, solution, or market.
- Existing alternatives, actual behavior, unresolved outcomes, and real usage matter.
- Hypothetical willingness to use or pay is not proof of demand.
- Natural comments and corrections from any Filipino participant may inform discovery, but no
  individual is a formal review gate or community representative.
- Legal, residence, employment, tax, and administrative facts must be checked against appropriate
  authoritative sources before they are presented as guidance.

## Initial discovery bounds

A provisional first discovery round may aim for about 50 completed surveys and about 10 substantive
Filipino conversations or follow-ups. A later employer track may include about 5 conversations if
the emerging evidence makes that useful.

These figures only bound planning effort. They are not targets that prove success, validation
thresholds, or evidence of demand, and they may change as the pilots teach us more.
